# Swiss Style

## 1. Definition

Swiss Style, also known as the International Typographic Style, is a modernist graphic design movement that developed in Switzerland during the 1950s. It focuses on creating clear, simple, and organized designs that communicate information effectively.

## 2. Characteristics

- Clean and minimal layouts
- Use of grid systems
- Sans-serif typography
- Asymmetrical layouts
- Strong organization and alignment
- Focus on readability and communication

## 3. Imagery

Swiss Style designs often feature organized grids, large photographs, simple shapes, and clean typography. Posters usually have plenty of open space and carefully arranged text.

## 4. Color Scheme

Swiss Style often uses simple and limited color palettes. Black, white, and red are common because they create strong contrast and keep the design clean and easy to read.

## 5. Examples

- Josef Müller-Brockmann's poster designs
- Helvetica typography
- Swiss International Style posters from the 1950s and 1960s
