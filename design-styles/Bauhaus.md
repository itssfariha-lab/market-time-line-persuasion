# Bauhaus

## 1. Definition

Bauhaus is a modernist design movement that began with the Bauhaus school founded by Walter Gropius in Germany in 1919. The movement focused on combining art, design, craftsmanship, and technology to create designs that were simple, functional, and useful.

## 2. Characteristics

- Simple geometric shapes
- Clean and minimal designs
- Focus on function rather than decoration
- Use of modern and industrial materials
- Sans-serif typography and organized layouts
- Combination of art, technology, and craftsmanship

## 3. Imagery

Bauhaus designs commonly use geometric shapes such as circles, squares, rectangles, and triangles. Posters, furniture, architecture, and typography from the movement often have simple layouts and very little unnecessary decoration.

## 4. Color Scheme

Bauhaus design is commonly associated with primary colors such as red, blue, and yellow, along with black and white. These bold colors work well with the simple geometric forms commonly found in Bauhaus designs.

## 5. Examples

- The Bauhaus building in Dessau designed by Walter Gropius
- Marcel Breuer's modern furniture designs
- Herbert Bayer's Bauhaus typography and poster designs
