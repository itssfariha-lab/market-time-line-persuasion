# New Wave Typography

## 1. Definition

New Wave Typography is a postmodern graphic design movement that became popular during the 1970s and 1980s. It rejected the strict rules and organized layouts of Swiss Style and experimented with typography, spacing, and unusual page layouts.

## 2. Characteristics

- Experimental typography
- Irregular spacing and letter placement
- Text placed at different angles
- Layered images and text
- Asymmetrical layouts
- Breaking traditional grid systems

## 3. Imagery

New Wave Typography often uses text as a major visual element. Letters may appear tilted, stretched, layered, or placed in unexpected positions to make the design more expressive.

## 4. Color Scheme

New Wave Typography does not have one required color palette. Designers often use bold and contrasting colors to make typography stand out and create energetic compositions.

## 5. Examples

- Wolfgang Weingart's experimental typography
- April Greiman's graphic design
- Posters that combine photography with experimental type layouts
